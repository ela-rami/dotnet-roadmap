# How this course works

## The goal

Become a backend C#/.NET developer who can **explain** and **write by hand** everything a junior role expects — not just use it with an AI's help.

## A lesson at a time

There's no fixed syllabus to march through. The [course map](course-map.md) lists everything that must be covered by the end; the **next lesson is chosen each time**, based on:

- how the previous lesson and its exercises went
- my comments on the lessons
- what is getting rusty and is worth revisiting
- the end goal: interviews for junior backend .NET roles

Things I already know still get a lesson — revising doesn't hurt — but those lessons go faster.

## What a lesson looks like

1. **Goals** — what I'll be able to do at the end.
2. **Explanation** — one concept at a time, with concrete examples (often from manufacturing and machine data, the domain I work in).
3. **"In an interview"** boxes — the typical question on the topic and what a good answer sounds like.
4. **Common mistakes.**
5. **Summary.**
6. **Exercises** — written by me, by hand, without AI or copy-paste. Then a review in the chat.
7. **Explain it** — short questions I answer in my own words, in English, as in an interview.
8. **Official docs** — links to Microsoft Learn for deeper reading.

At the end of each section: a **quiz** and a small realistic **assignment** that combines what the section covered.

## Rules

- **I write the code.** The tutor explains, gives hints and reviews — never the solution before I've tried.
- If I'm stuck: first a hint in words, then a more precise hint, only at the end the solution.
- After every exercise I **explain what I wrote**, as I would in an interview.
- **Clarifying questions first:** when a task is vague, I ask before coding.
- SQL is written in **T-SQL (SQL Server)**.
